STORY SELECTION & THE INTERVIEW

Story selection
Choose a guest you and Caleb agree on. Have a pre-chat about their work — ask what is new or exciting
them. Their latest passion is often the topic. You do not need the topic to be the whole interview; it is
the hook that gets the listener to tune in.

The interview
Prepare 10–12 simple, open-ended fallback questions that start with How, Why, or What. But the most
important thing is to LISTEN. The best interviews sound like two people talking over a fence — engage,
laugh, probe. Pauses are fine; we tighten dead spots in post.

The script
Keep it simple, direct, and engaging — a teaser, not a summary. The format is about three clips with your
voice linking them and music as a bridge. One thought, one sentence. Stay in present tense and add your

personality. Submit your draft to Caleb and the Producer; they will tweak it and send it back for your
final revision.

The taping
Record in a smallish room without high ceilings — carpet and heavy drapes help deaden the sound.
Stand if you can, use your hands, smile, and ad-lib parts so it sounds natural rather than read.

Pre-interview chat tips
    •​ Get to know your guest and agree on talking points and topics.
    •​ Emphasize a quiet space. Common culprits: pets, people in adjacent rooms, dishes, fans.
    •​ Check equipment. Everyone should use closed-back headphones or earbuds; avoid inline earbud
       mics as the primary mic.
    •​ Ask guests not to click pens or tap desks, and to silence notifications.

Riverside Studio Checklist
    •​ Set the correct time zone (Riverside defaults to UTC).
    •​ Confirm the correct mic and headphones are selected for input/output (check Audio MIDI
       settings or restart if needed).

    •​ Everyone on closed-back headphones. If someone has none, have them click “I am not wearing
       headphones” and enable echo cancellation.
    •​ Enable “remove background noise” if needed; use “low data mode” if the connection struggles;
       wired ethernet beats wifi.
    •​ Hit RECORD. Capture the guest intro soundbite: “This is ____, and you are listening to The
       Avalanche Hour Podcast.”
    •​ Take a pause at a natural break for the mid-roll ad.
    •​ Before anyone leaves: confirm both tracks fully upload (turn off mic/camera but leave the
       browser open until done).
    •​ Download both .WAV files, upload to your episode Drive folder, and save a local copy.

Recording Best Practices (Lessons Learned)
    •​ Room: smallish, low ceilings, carpet and heavy drapes to deaden sound.
    •​ Quiet: no pets, adjacent talkers, dishes, or fans; silence notifications; no pen-clicking or
       desk-tapping.
    •​ Mics: avoid inline earbud mics as the primary (they rustle on clothing and hair); closed-back
       headphones for everyone to prevent feedback.
    •​ Prep: a pre-interview chat a few days ahead relaxes the guest and aligns talking points.
    •​ Support: if you want a producer to sit in on the first few minutes to check sound quality, note it
       in the Mastermind sheet.

Mic kits, specs & “Where’s My Mic?”
We ship mobile recording kits in pelican cases. Request one a few weeks before you need it; when
finished, tape it back up and send it on with the provided label.
Minimum equipment (draft):
    •​ Preferred: a TAH mobile kit, or a dedicated USB mic (e.g., Rode NT-USB / Rode Podcaster).
    •​ Acceptable: Rode wired headset mic, or Rode Wireless GO with a lav.
    •​ Everyone on closed-back headphones or earbuds (never laptop speakers — they cause echo).
    •​ Avoid as primary: built-in laptop mic and inline earbud mics.
    •​ Stable internet (ethernet preferred) and a quiet, soft-furnished room.

POST-PRODUCTION & DELIVERY

First cut
At minimum, listen to the recording and mark timestamps and edit notes (for example: “7:28 cut the
long pause and umm”; “cut 23:32 to 25:41”). Pull 2–3 soundbites for the open, or just give the producer
the timestamps. Do this shortly after recording, while it is fresh.

Show notes
A 2–3 paragraph, human-written summary; topics covered; a brief guest bio; relevant links to their
company or organization; a high-quality cover photo of the guest; and music and photo credit.
