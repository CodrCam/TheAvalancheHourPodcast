# Host and Producer Demo Videos — Shoot Plan

This plan covers the two videos to record first:

1. **Host Studio: from assignment to producer handoff**
2. **Producer workflow: set up and run an Episode Studio**

The host video should answer, “Where do I go, what do I owe, and how do I hand it off?” The producer video should answer, “How do I create the operating plan, confirm readiness, and move the episode through production?”

## Keep the role boundary clear

| Video | Include | Save for a later video |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Host | Home, Resources, Follow-ups, My Episodes, Episode Package, Production Board, Guest Questionnaire, My Profile, Mic Kits, notifications, support, submission, and proof approval | Store fulfillment, inventory control, team permissions, and system settings |
| Producer | Home triage, Episode Calendar, Studio creation, recording schedule, guest intake, mic readiness, Production Board, checklist setup, sponsor reads, host preview, handoff review, proof workflow, and production follow-ups | Store/shipping operations and Caleb-only access/permission administration |

The website is the production system of record, but it does not replace Riverside. It records the confirmed session details and room link; the producer still creates the correct Riverside production, studio, and scheduled session, invites the guest, and confirms that the host and guest are online and recording in Riverside.

## Before recording

### Accounts and demo records

- Record the host video while signed in to an account that has only normal host access. This is the clearest proof of what hosts can actually see.
- Record the producer video with the producer/Studio manager account.
- Prepare two safe demo Episode Studios:
  - **Fresh setup:** used to demonstrate creating and configuring an episode.
  - **Ready for review:** already contains sample guest details, a submitted questionnaire, mic plans, a few safe files, one known gap, and a private proof.
- Use fictional guest details, a non-working example email, and fake mailing/tracking information.
- Prepare three small, harmless demo files with clear names: one WAV, one JPG, and one PDF. Never expose a real guest address, customer order, password, private proof, or unreleased Spotify link.

Suggested training episode:

- Title: `Training Demo — Early Season Snowpack`
- Season: `Season 11`
- Air date: at least 35 days in the future, so the default workflow does not begin overdue
- Host package due: allow the automatic ten-days-before-air date, then explain that it can be changed
- Recording: a future date, 60 minutes, correct time zone
- Recording location: a safe demo link or visible text such as `Riverside — Training Studio`

### Recording setup

- Capture at 1920×1080 with the browser at 100% zoom.
- Hide bookmarks, personal browser tabs, notifications, password-manager prompts, and the desktop menu bar if possible.
- Use a clean cursor path and pause for roughly one second after each click.
- Record in short chapters. A clean edit made from eight small takes will be easier to follow than one rushed fifteen-minute take.
- When a control would send an email, issue a live guest link, upload a real file, or change production data, either use the safe demo record or stop immediately before the final action and explain the result.

## Video 1 — Host Studio: from assignment to producer handoff

**Target length:** 11–13 minutes  
**Viewer outcome:** A host can find an assignment, prepare the guest and recording, provide the required material, submit the package, and respond to proof review without needing a private tutorial.

### 0:00–0:35 — Opening and orientation

**Screen:** Sign in, then land on **Home**.

**Narration:**

> This is the Avalanche Hour Team Studio. As a host, this is where you find your assignments, deadlines, guest preparation, recording details, mic-kit status, episode files, and producer feedback. If you remember only one thing, make the Episode Studio—not email or text messages—the source of truth for the episode.

### 0:35–1:30 — Home, priorities, notifications, and help

**Show:** The Today view, next actions, active episodes, quick links, notification bell, and **Technical help**.

**Narration:**

> Home is your daily starting point. The top of the page summarizes what needs attention and gives you the fastest paths into your episodes, resources, profile, and mic kits. The notification bell collects episode changes, sponsor-read updates, mic-kit activity, and reminders. If the website, account, or recording tools are the problem, use Technical help—start with the team WhatsApp chat so the answer can help everyone, and use Private text support for something sensitive or urgent.

**Tip on screen:** `Start on Home; work from the next action, not from memory.`

### 1:30–2:15 — Resource Center

**Show:** **Resources**, the host pathway, search, suggested searches, and the field manual.

**Narration:**

> Resources is the operating guide. Follow the host pathway for the full sequence, or search for the immediate problem—for example, setting up Riverside, checking audio, waiting for an upload, preparing a guest, or requesting a mic kit. Use the guide before inventing a new process, because it reflects the current season’s workflow.

### 2:15–3:15 — My Episodes and requesting an idea

**Show:** **My Episodes**, summary counts, calendar, episode cards, and **Request an episode**.

**Narration:**

> My Episodes contains only the work connected to you as a host, producer, creator, or workflow owner. The summary shows what is in progress, with the producer, accepted, or off track. You can also request an episode idea here. That request becomes a tracked team follow-up; it does not create a scheduled Episode Studio by itself.

**Tip on screen:** `“Off track” is a forward-looking risk signal—not a grade.`

### 3:15–4:25 — Episode header, schedule, and delivery outlook

**Show:** Open an episode. Point out the status, air date, host-package due date, recording session, **Open recording room**, **Add to calendar**, completion, and **Delivery outlook**.

**Narration:**

> At the top of every Episode Studio, confirm the recording date, time, time zone, room link, air date, and the earlier host-package deadline. Add the session to your calendar. Delivery outlook is separate from checklist completion: mark the episode off track as soon as the schedule is at risk, even if you do not yet have the full explanation. Add the context in the episode conversation once you know it.

### 4:25–5:20 — Communication Clipboard

**Show:** Pinned producer direction and the dated team conversation.

**Narration:**

> The Communication Clipboard keeps two different kinds of information together. The pinned note is the producer’s current direction. The conversation is the dated record for episode decisions, questions, handoffs, and changes. Keep credentials and sensitive guest information out of both. Technical problems belong in the support channel, not in the episode record.

### 5:20–6:25 — Guest Questionnaire

**Show:** Open **Guest Questionnaire**. Show the welcome, the pre-interview and interview scheduling links, visible questions, uploads, guest-link state, and submitted response state.

**Narration:**

> Each episode has its own private guest questionnaire. Review the welcome, scheduling links, questions, and requested files before sharing it. Creating a link does not mean it was sent, so use Mark link as shared only after you actually send it. When the guest submits, review the response and use the safe fill action for blank Studio fields. It will not overwrite newer team edits, and private shipping information stays restricted.

**Tip on screen:** `A replacement guest link invalidates the old one and reopens the “sent” task.`

### 6:25–8:25 — Complete the Episode Package

**Show:** Return to **Episode package**. Move through the checklist without opening every field. Demonstrate one written response, one file upload area, guest details, microphone plan, timestamped edit notes, photo selection, and sponsor-read evidence.

**Narration:**

> The checklist is the official producer handoff. Put finished writing in its matching step, timestamped edit directions only in the edit-notes step, and files directly in the requirement they satisfy. The microphone plan must cover every assigned host and the guest. For files, use a consistent episode name, the asset, the person or description, and a version—for example, `early-snowpack_interview_guest-01_raw.wav`. For an edit, name the exact file, start and end time, requested action, and intended result. For photos, choose the final three in order and include crop, caption, credit, permission, and restrictions.

> Files in Episode Studio are temporary production storage and expire after 180 days. Keep permanent masters in the approved archive.

**Tips on screen:** 

- `Do not upload “audio-final-final.wav.”`
- `Do not leave Riverside until every participant shows Upload Complete.`
- `Questions go in the conversation; deliverables go in the checklist.`

### 8:25–9:20 — Save and submit

**Show:** The save bar, missing-item explanations, **Send to producer**, and **Send with known gaps** states.

**Narration:**

> Save drafts as you work. A complete package can be sent when all required items are satisfied. If production needs to start early, Send with known gaps becomes available only after every missing requirement is acknowledged with a resolution plan and the producer directions are complete. This creates a visible, accountable exception; it is not permission to leave the package vague.

### 9:20–10:15 — Production Board and private proof

**Show:** **Production board**, Board filters, a host-owned task, and the private proof section.

**Narration:**

> The Production Board answers who owes what and when. Filter to Mine to find your tasks. Open a card to see its dependencies, deadline, note, and linked package requirements. Later, the producer uploads a private proof here. Download and listen to the complete program, then either approve that exact version or record precise requested changes. Never forward the private Studio proof or staged publishing link to a guest.

### 10:15–11:10 — Mic Kits

**Show:** **Mic Kits**, availability, **I need a mic kit**, episode selection, need-by date, private address, status, tracking, and **I received this kit** if a safe demo is available.

**Narration:**

> Request equipment as soon as the recording date is taking shape—even if every kit currently looks checked out. Tie the request to the correct episode, give a realistic need-by date, and enter the private delivery address in the protected form. When it arrives, confirm receipt in the Studio, test the exact microphone and headphones you will use, and keep the request status current instead of starting a separate email chain.

### 11:10–12:10 — Follow-ups, profile, and close

**Show:** **Follow-ups**, then **My Profile** and **View public profile**.

**Narration:**

> Use Follow-ups for a blocker, question, decision, or durable next step that needs an owner beyond one episode conversation. Use My Profile for your own public biography and approved photos; guest information belongs in the Episode Studio. Publish profile changes only after checking the public result.

> Your normal rhythm is simple: start on Home, work from My Episodes, keep decisions and files inside the Episode Studio, flag schedule risk early, and use the Producer Board and notifications to close the loop.

## Video 2 — Producer workflow: set up and run an Episode Studio

**Target length:** 13–16 minutes  
**Viewer outcome:** A producer can create the production record, confirm the host and guest are ready, manage deadlines and requirements, review the handoff, and move the episode through proof and publishing.

### 0:00–0:35 — Producer responsibility

**Screen:** Producer account on **Home**.

**Narration:**

> The producer owns the operating plan: the right people, the right dates, a clear guest intake, a tested recording setup, visible deadlines, an unambiguous host package, and a reviewed final proof. The Team Studio coordinates that work; Riverside records it.

### 0:35–1:20 — Triage from Home

**Show:** Priority queue, off-track work, mic-kit items, episode follow-ups, notifications, and quick links.

**Narration:**

> Start on Home and work from priority order. Check off-track episodes, overdue production steps, guest or mic-kit blockers, and new host handoffs before opening the calendar. Notifications tell you what changed; the episode itself remains the source of truth.

### 1:20–3:10 — Create an Episode Studio

**Show:** **Episode Calendar** → **New Episode Studio**. Fill the working title, season, air date, host-package due date, recording date, time, time zone, duration, recording location, producer, producer notification email, and assigned hosts.

**Narration:**

> Create the Studio as soon as the episode is real enough to schedule. The air date places it on the season calendar and automatically proposes a host-package deadline ten days earlier. Confirm the recording date, start time, time zone, length, and room link. Select the actual producer and every host who must work in the package. If the recording schedule is not confirmed, leave the whole recording block blank rather than saving a partial time or missing time zone.

**Tips on screen:**

- `Air date drives the default production deadlines.`
- `Every co-host who must edit the package needs to be assigned.`
- `“Access pending” means the person still needs an account connection; save that fix for the admin workflow.`

### 3:10–4:05 — Riverside setup and day-of attendance

**Show:** Briefly show the Resource Center’s Riverside instructions, then return to the Episode Studio recording summary.

**Narration:**

> Outside the website, open the approved Avalanche Hour Riverside account, use the correct existing production and studio, create the scheduled session, set the visible time zone, and invite the interview subject as a Guest. Put the confirmed room link and session details back in Episode Studio. On recording day, the producer must still verify inside Riverside that the host and guest are online, the intended microphones and headphones are selected, the test recording sounds clean, and every participant is recording and uploading. Episode Studio does not detect live attendance.

### 4:05–5:00 — Verify Settings and preview the host experience

**Show:** **Episode settings**, save, then **View as host**.

**Narration:**

> After creation, open Settings and verify the title, air date, host deadline, recording details, producer, notification email, and host assignments. Then use View as host. It is a read-only quality check, not a permission change. Confirm that the host sees clear dates, instructions, and requirements before asking them to begin.

### 5:00–6:35 — Configure and send the Guest Questionnaire

**Show:** **Guest Questionnaire**. Demonstrate the welcome, two booking steps, question visibility and required state, resume/photo handoff, save, create link, copy, and **Mark link as shared**. Then switch to a submitted demo response and show review plus safe autofill.

**Narration:**

> Tailor the episode-specific questionnaire before issuing its link. Connect both booking steps when you use them: the pre-interview conversation and the actual interview recording. Review which questions and uploads are visible and required. Save before creating the private link, then mark it shared only after it has actually been sent. When the response arrives, review the guest’s internet, microphone, headphones, quiet room, promotion consent, files, and scheduling acknowledgements. Apply the response to blank Episode Studio fields; current team edits are preserved and restricted shipping answers remain out of the package.

### 6:35–8:25 — Build the Production Board

**Show:** **Production board** in Board view, search and filters, phase progress, a task drawer, Start work/Mark complete/Waive, add/edit task, and Schedule view.

**Narration:**

> The Production Board is the accountability layer. Board view groups the work into Host preparation, Producer review, Publishing, and Release coordination. Filter to Open, Mine, or Overdue; open a task to see dependencies, linked package requirements, the working note, evidence, deadline, owner, and history. Producers can add or edit episode-specific tasks, while managers can deliberately waive a requirement with attribution. Schedule view puts every deadline and accountable owner on one screen. Changing the air date moves unfinished tasks that still use their standard offset; deliberate overrides and completed work keep their recorded dates.

**Tip on screen:** `Use Package for the words and files; use Production Board for owner, deadline, and status.`

### 8:25–9:55 — Customize the host package and sponsor requirements

**Show:** **Episode package**, **Customize checklist**, add/edit/reorder/required-optional behavior, save, sponsor-read assignment, and pinned producer note.

**Narration:**

> Before the host is deep into the assignment, customize the checklist for this episode. Each requirement should tell the host what completion means and whether the response is writing, a file, or an optional source link. Assign only the current approved sponsor-read snapshot and choose whether evidence is a separate ad file or included in the main recording. Use the pinned producer note for the current direction and the conversation for dated decisions.

### 9:55–11:05 — Producer readiness preflight

**Show:** Episode summary, questionnaire status, microphone plan, Mic Kits status, recording link, and Board tasks.

**Narration:**

> Before recording, confirm six things: the host and producer assignments are correct; the date, time zone, duration, and room link are final; the guest has received and completed the intake; both booking steps are confirmed; every host and the guest has a tested microphone and headphones or an active mic-kit plan; and Riverside shows the correct people online with clean test audio. If any one of those is uncertain, flag the episode off track and create or update the accountable task.

### 11:05–12:35 — Review the host handoff

**Show:** Submitted package, assembled producer files, known-gap panel, edit directions, photos, sponsor evidence, **Request changes**, and **Accept package**.

**Narration:**

> A submitted package is locked against silent host changes. Review the guest details and profiles, mic readiness, sponsor evidence, raw tracks, edit directions, show-notes source, final photo choices, credits, permissions, and every known gap. Download from the assembled Producer package instead of hunting through external folders. Request Changes requires specific feedback and reopens the existing package without erasing it. Accept only when the material is ready for the next production stage.

**Tip on screen:** `A known gap must have an owner and resolution plan; do not move it back into private chat.`

### 12:35–13:45 — Private proof, approval, and publishing handoff

**Show:** Production Board proof section, upload/replacement behavior, host approval, publishing and promotion tasks, and staged listen field if appropriate.

**Narration:**

> Upload the private producer proof with a clear versioned filename. The assigned host downloads and approves that exact stored version or requests precise changes. Replacing the proof automatically reopens approval and downstream publishing checks. Never send the secured Studio proof, internal package, or staged Spotify link to a guest. If an advance guest listen is intentionally approved, make a separate permission-controlled copy containing only the approved program.

### 13:45–14:40 — Follow-ups and close

**Show:** **Follow-ups**, Resources producer pathway, and Home.

**Narration:**

> Use Follow-ups when a blocker, decision, or cross-team next step needs durable ownership outside one episode conversation. Use the producer pathway in Resources for the current operating procedure. The producer loop is: build the Studio, verify guest and recording readiness, make every deadline and requirement explicit, review the handoff, upload and resolve the proof, and close the publishing tasks before air.

## Producer preflight card for the edit

Use this as a full-screen title card or downloadable checklist near the end of the producer video:

### Before the recording starts

- Episode Studio exists and the correct host(s) and producer are assigned.
- Recording date, local time, time zone, length, and room link are correct.
- The correct Riverside production, studio, and scheduled session exist.
- Guest was invited with the Guest role, not a producer or host role.
- Pre-interview and recording times are confirmed.
- Guest questionnaire is shared and reviewed.
- Host and guest mic/headphone plans are ready; any kit is received and tested.
- Host and guest are online in Riverside and the intended inputs are selected.
- A 15-second test has been recorded and listened to.
- Producer can see that every participant is recording and uploading.

### Before anyone leaves Riverside

- Recording is stopped deliberately.
- Every participant shows **Upload Complete** or **Successfully uploaded**.
- Separate WAV tracks are downloaded when available.
- A local working backup exists.
- Final files are attached to the matching Episode Studio requirements.
- The relevant Production Board task and episode conversation are updated.

## Editing notes for both videos

- Open each video with the outcome, not a list of menus.
- Use short chapter cards: **Find the work**, **Prepare**, **Record**, **Hand off**, **Review**.
- Add a subtle cursor highlight, but avoid click animations that cover labels.
- Speed up typing and file-transfer footage; keep decisions, warnings, and result states at normal speed.
- When a form is long, demonstrate one representative field and summarize the rest in voiceover.
- Blur or crop all email addresses, phone numbers, street addresses, tracking numbers, private guest links, unreleased program links, and account identifiers.
- End each video with the viewer’s normal daily loop, not with an edge-case control.
